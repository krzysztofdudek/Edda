# 0. One agent and a request

**You're here if** you write a request, take what comes back, and check it yourself by clicking through it or reading the result.

**The idea:** I have one agent and I ask it to do this and that for me. It does more than answer: it does the work on my computer.

**Stands on:** nothing. This is where the ladder starts.

## The story

On a Sunday at the end of August I was working with one agent on one of my tools. I also keep a few AI models on my laptop to try things out, and some of them are huge. I wanted some disk space back, so I gave it one line: "Throw out the downloaded models, we're cleaning the disk."

That was the whole request. The agent looked through the disk, found the models, and deleted the ones it took for junk. All of it happened on my machine, between my message and its report.

One of the models it deleted was one I wanted to keep. That part is chapter 1.

*I tell this from the notes my agent kept that day. The session itself is no longer on my machine.*

## The idea

A chat answer is text. I read it and decide what to do with it. An agent runs commands, edits files and deletes things on my machine. It writes code, runs it, reads what broke and fixes it, the way a person would. It does all of that between my message and its report.

## What I do

For small things, one request is still all I need, and I keep it that simple on purpose. A small fix gets a quick fix and my usual checks, with none of the ceremony I use for a new feature.

I check the result the simplest way there is. When I can try a change myself, I skip the film an agent would record of it: "I don't need a film, I'll click it myself."

## Try it tomorrow

Nothing to install.

1. Open the last session you had with your agent and find your request in it, word for word.
2. Scroll past the agent's final summary and list what it did on your machine: the commands it ran, and the files it created, changed or deleted. In a repository, `git status` and `git diff` show the files. Anything it did outside the repository, only the session shows.
3. Put the two side by side: your request on one side, the list on the other.

## What changes

You see how much work one of your sentences turned into on your own machine, and how much of it you only find in the session's record. Keep the list. Chapter 1 starts from it.

## Where it stops

Everything I didn't say, the agent filled in with something that looked right to it. That's step 1. And every new session starts from zero: tomorrow the agent remembers none of today. That's step 5.

## When your own hands stop being enough

Not yet. At this step the agent you already use is all you need.
