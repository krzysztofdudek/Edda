# Episode 1. Throw out the junk

*How far can you let agents go?*, episode 1. It tells steps 0 and 1 of the ladder.

**The film:** [Throw out the junk, on YouTube](https://youtu.be/RM6NRcV47PU)

## What it tells

On a Sunday at the end of August I asked an agent to throw out the downloaded models on my laptop. It found one model of 73 GB that it hadn't expected and that we had never used. It took those two notes as its reason and deleted it. I had picked that version and downloaded it myself. There was no copy, so the only way back was to download all 73 GB again. I had never said which models were junk, and the agent filled that part in by itself.

Four weeks later one program on my laptop had grown to a hundred gigabytes, and I asked an agent to find out what was there. It came back with a list first. Next to most of the points it wrote what it would leave alone, and it waited for my answer. It doesn't go like this every time. The night it doesn't is the story of step 15.

## Where it sits

Step 0 is the setting: one agent and a request, and the agent does the work on my own computer. [Chapter 0, One agent and a request](../chapters/00-one-agent-and-a-request.md).

## The tool at this step

[Urd](https://github.com/krzysztofdudek/UrdSkill) is the tool I use at step 1. When an agent is about to act on something it could read two ways, Urd makes it look up the source of truth or ask me, instead of guessing. It appears in the film only on the end plate. It wasn't the thing that could have saved the model. I built it in May, months before that Sunday, and back then it woke up only when an agent moved from a plan into code. Cleaning my disk had no plan. In October I widened it to every request an agent could read two ways. "Throw out the junk" is one of those. I'm still checking whether it now wakes up where it should.

## What in the film is what

**My words.** Six cards carry my own words, translated from Polish word for word. Four come from session transcripts: my two messages of 26 September, my sentence about who writes my code and how I check it, and "Tell me if you know what I mean. Tell me, and I'll confirm." The two lines of 30 August survive only in the entry my agent wrote in my diary that evening. That entry quotes them as mine, and I confirmed both for the film. Where a card leaves out a name, its note says so.

**The agent's words.** Three cards carry the agent's own words, and nobody reads them aloud. The one marked FROM THE RECORD is its diary entry of 30 August. The two marked RECONSTRUCTED are its messages of 26 and 27 September, redrawn as cards from the session record. They aren't recordings of my screen. All three are translated from Polish and shortened where the card shows "…".

**Told from one source.** 30 August comes only from that diary entry, written by the agent that did the deleting. The session itself is no longer on my machine. So the film has no clock times for that day and no list of the other models the agent removed. It has none of the agent's messages from the session either, only its own account of them afterwards. Whether I ever downloaded the model again isn't on record either.

**Drawn.** Every picture is an illustration. The garage is the laptop's disk, a box is a file, the dumpster is deletion, the sealed boxes with round windows are containers, and the robots are the agents: One is the agent of 30 August, Two the agent of September, in a different session. What the robots do follows the record. How they move, the jokes and the garage itself are made up for the picture. The STOP card One pins on the door frame stands for the rule it wrote in my diary, and the film doesn't claim that Two ever read it.

**Synthetic voices.** The narrator is a synthetic voice, and so is the second voice that reads my cards. Neither is mine. An agent wrote the narration from the record, a second model checked it against the record, and I approved every word. The narrator speaks about me in the third person, as "Chris".

**Left out.** The model's name, the program's name, what the two gigabytes of files I named myself were, and the other projects in the agent's September lists. One card comes from my work with a team, so it shows no date and no context.
